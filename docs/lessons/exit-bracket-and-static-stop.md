# Lesson: exit execution = resting GTC bracket, with a STATIC stop

Hand-authored (not generated by `scripts/build_alpha_memory.py`). Captures a
settled decision so the trailing-vs-static question is not re-litigated.

## The execution model the backtester assumes

Every position is modeled as:

- **Entry**: market-on-open the next session (`apply_entry_fill` on the open).
- **Stop**: a resting **SELL STOP** at `stop_price`. Triggered when the daily
  **low** reaches it; filled at the stop (or the open on a gap-down).
- **Target**: a resting **SELL LIMIT** at `target_price`. Triggered when the
  daily **high** reaches it; filled at the target (or the open on a gap-up).

So `daily_high`/`daily_low` are **fill detectors for resting orders**, not
look-ahead — you are filled at the *level*, never at the high/low. This is only
realistic if those resting GTC bracket orders actually exist at the broker. If
you instead act EOD on the report without resting orders, the target/stop fills
are phantom and EV is overstated. (`quant/position_manager.py` evaluate_exit_signals,
`quant/backtester.py` ~1987/1993/2948, `quant/fill_model.py` apply_stop/target_fill.)

## Static stop beats trailing — decided, do not re-test

`exp-20260623-020` (and earlier `exp-20260503-009`): on the canonical 3 windows,
a **static stop frozen at entry** (`avg_cost - 1.5*ATR-at-entry`) is the
EV-optimal policy. ATR-trailing loses in **every** window:

| stop policy | 3-window EV sum | vs static |
|---|---|---|
| static (frozen entry) | 7.8941 | baseline |
| trail trigger 3 / offset 2 ATR | 5.5377 | -2.36 (-30%) |
| trail trigger 4 / offset 2 ATR | 3.4325 | -4.46 (-57%) |

Worst-drawdown is ~the same (~11%) either way, so trailing buys **no** risk
reduction — it only cuts winners short before the 3.5x ATR target. **Set the
protective stop once at entry; do NOT trail it up.** Do not re-run ATR-trailing
sweeps; only revisit with a genuinely different mechanism (regime-conditional
trail, percent-of-MFE giveback, forward live giveback data).

Trade-off to accept: a static stop sits far below market on a big winner
(e.g. a 3x position protected only at `entry-1.5*ATR`) — minimal single-name
protection. The EV win comes from letting winners run; you must tolerate the
single-name give-back.

## What ships from this

`quant/bracket_orders.py` + `run.py` write a daily **OUTPUT-ONLY** playbook
(`data/daily/orders/bracket_orders_<date>.json` + a report section): per
position, the resting `SELL LIMIT @ target` and `SELL STOP @ static entry stop`
to place/maintain. run.py reconstructs the static entry stop from history
(`avg_cost - 1.5*ATR-at-entry`) since `open_positions.stop_price` has been
trailed; falls back to the trailed stop (with a warning) when history is
unavailable. run.py never submits orders — the operator places them.
