# exp-20260615-023 Efficiency-Ratio Chop Axis + Exposure Scalar

Status: `observed_only`  Lane: `measurement_repair`  Builds on: `exp-20260615-019`

## Headline conclusion: ER REJECTED as the chop axis

Kaufman Efficiency Ratio on SPY does NOT reproduce or improve the exp-019
"choppy_range loses" separation — it arguably inverts it:

- FGRS by ER tercile: low-ER(chop) +$414, mid +$279, high-ER(trend) +$419 —
  non-monotonic (middle worst). Spearman(ER, PnL) = +0.105 (weak).
- Deferred by ER tercile: low-ER(chop) +$139 (best), mid +$67, high-ER(trend)
  +$38 (worst). Spearman(ER, PnL) = -0.114 (reversed vs the hypothesis).
- Why: the ER day distribution shows `late_strong` — the STRONGEST window —
  had the LOWEST mean ER (0.157; 89% of days < 0.30). A steady grind-up has low
  index path-efficiency, so ER mislabels the best window as "choppy."

Lesson: the industry ER / Choppiness-Index convention does NOT transfer to this
stock-selection context. The exp-019 loss axis ("choppy_range" = SPY near/below
200d MA + weak breadth, but NOT stressed) is a 2D trend-state x breadth
construct, which is a different thing from index 20d path-efficiency. ER on the
index conflates "low net move with many wiggles" (which steady bull grinds also
show) with the structurally-below-trend market that actually hurt these
strategies. RETAIN the exp-019 trend-state+breadth regime label as the better
separator; do NOT substitute ER. The continuous exposure-scalar idea is still
valid in principle, but it must be driven by the exp-019 regime construct, not
by index ER.
