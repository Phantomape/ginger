# Experiment Card: exp-20260612-020

## Summary

The mixed|balanced|normal state tilt on the accepted consensus sleeve (exp-20260612-018: +7.4% aggregate EV, zero regression, improved drawdown, rejected only on APP single-ticker concentration) becomes acceptable when the tilt carries a per-ticker repetition cap: only a ticker's first N in-cell entries within a trailing 60-trading-day window receive the 1.5x paper notional tilt, diluting single-name dependence below the 50% concentration guard.

## Post-Run Reflection

- Why did the result happen? The per-ticker trailing-60-trading-day repetition cap backfired
  directionally. APP's 11 in-cell entries are temporally dispersed (it re-qualifies across
  months, so the rolling cap rarely binds it), while diversifier names like MU cluster inside
  one window and were capped hard. Both predeclared concentration guards therefore got WORSE
  than the uniform tilt: in-cell single-ticker positive share 51.4% -> 52.8% (cap 3) / 53.2%
  (cap 2), tilt-incremental share 56.0% / 58.6%. EV/PnL/drawdown stayed clean (best variant
  +0.7101 aggregate EV, +$15,276, three windows improved, zero regressed, DD improved), so the
  rejection is purely concentration -- and the deeper finding is that the cell's apparent
  regime edge is substantially a persistent single-name (APP) effect, not a weighting artifact.
- Which near-neighbor retry is now forbidden? Any further weighting-rule engineering of this
  tilt on the frozen windows: per-window caps, absolute per-ticker notional ceilings, ticker
  exclusion lists, scalar changes, merged cells. Explicitly forbidden: an ex-APP version of the
  tilt (ticker-specific exception from few trades). Two consecutive concentration rejections on
  the same 39 rows make further variants guard p-hacking.
- What new evidence would justify a retry? (a) Forward closed consensus-sleeve rows in this
  state that organically dilute APP below 50% of in-cell positive PnL, then rerun the simple
  uniform tilt of exp-20260612-018 unchanged; or (b) a new router-candidate screening criterion
  (606-022 style) requiring the cell edge to survive excluding its top ticker before any tilt
  experiment is reserved, applied to other sleeve/state cells.
